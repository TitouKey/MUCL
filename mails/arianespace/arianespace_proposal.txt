Subject: Europe put the world's greatest telescope in the sky. Let us watch you do it again.

Dear Arianespace team,

One December night, in a clearing of the Guiana jungle, a rocket lifted off
carrying something the world had never built before: an eye pointed at the
beginning of the universe. The James Webb Space Telescope did not just leave
Earth — it left everything, to a point 1.5 million kilometres away, where it
now watches galaxies being born. And who sent it? Europe did. You did.

That is what Europe is in space. When the planet needed to trust its most
precious instrument to anyone, it trusted the teams at Kourou, the launch
service that has carried more of the world's space than almost anyone else on
Earth. Ariane is not just a rocket. It is the proof that Europe can do space
— completely, reliably, brilliantly.

We grew up watching those launches. And then we decided to do something about
it.

We are MARS UCLouvain, a student space programme from UCLouvain in Belgium.
Every two years, we send a crew of students to the Mars Desert Research
Station in the Utah desert — one of the most faithful Mars analogues on Earth.
For two weeks they live in isolation, run real experiments in health, biology,
geology and technology, and bring the mission back to classrooms across the
country. Since 2013, dozens of research projects have crossed the Atlantic
with us. We are not building rockets. But we are building something Europe
needs just as much: the next generation that will operate, design and launch
them — in French Guiana, in France, in every corner of the continent.

And that is where you come in.

Our request to you: as part of a partnership, would it be possible for our
crew to attend an Ariane 6 launch, or to visit the Guiana Space Centre at
Kourou — or even your facilities in France? We want our crew to stand, once,
where the European dream is actually fired into the sky, and to come back and
tell every classroom: it is real, it is ours, and it starts here. We want to
show that even from a Belgian campus, space is not a distant story — it is
Europe's story, and we are in it.

You sent humanity's greatest telescope to the stars. All we ask is a small
place in the grandstand to show the next generation what that looks like.

[Your name] — MARS UCLouvain
mars@uclouvain.be · Place de l'Université 1, 1348 Louvain-la-Neuve, Belgium
