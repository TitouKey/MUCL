Dear Telespazio team,

Every time an astronaut on the International Space Station talks to Earth, the signal does not fall from the sky. Somewhere below — at Fucino, at Malindi, in a control room lit by screens — someone is tracking, listening, answering. Before any mission is a rocket or a capsule, it is a ground station with a room full of brilliant, patient people who never leave.

That is your world. And it is exactly where our mission begins.

We are MARS UCLouvain, a student space programme from UCLouvain in Belgium. Every two years, we send a crew of students to the Mars Desert Research Station in the Utah desert — one of the most faithful Mars analogues on Earth. For two weeks they live in isolation, 8,000 kilometres from home, running real experiments in health, biology, geology and technology. Since 2013, dozens of research projects have crossed the Atlantic with us.

Here is the part you already know by heart: in the desert, the crew is helpless without the ground. The schedule, the data, the decisions, the human voice at the other end of the line — it all lives in a mission control room staffed by students on campus, the same way your engineers watch over satellites day and night. Astronauts are the story everyone sees. Telespazio is the reason the story can be told. Our mission works exactly the same way — just with fewer zeroes in the distance.

Behind every hero in space, there is a team on the ground. Be ours.
