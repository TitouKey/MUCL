Subject: Somewhere on Earth, a ground station is listening. We need it to listen to us.

Dear Telespazio team,

Every time an astronaut on the International Space Station talks to Earth, the
signal does not fall from the sky. Somewhere below — at Fucino, at Malindi, in
a control room lit by screens — someone is tracking, listening, answering.
Before any mission is a rocket or a capsule, it is a ground station with a
room full of brilliant, patient people who never leave.

That is your world. And it is exactly where our mission begins.

We are MARS UCLouvain, a student space programme from UCLouvain in Belgium.
Every two years, we send a crew of students to the Mars Desert Research
Station in the Utah desert — one of the most faithful Mars analogues on Earth.
For two weeks they live in isolation, 8,000 kilometres from home, running real
experiments in health, biology, geology and technology. Since 2013, dozens of
research projects have crossed the Atlantic with us.

Here is the part you already know by heart: in the desert, the crew is
helpless without the ground. The schedule, the data, the decisions, the human
voice at the other end of the line — it all lives in a mission control room
staffed by students on campus, the same way your engineers watch over
satellites day and night. Astronauts are the story everyone sees. Telespazio
is the reason the story can be told. Our mission works exactly the same way —
just with fewer zeroes in the distance.

Our challenge to you: be our ground station. Join the mission as the 
telemetry and mission-support partner, connect the desert to the classroom —
or, more simply, put your name on the mission jersey and help us show a new
generation the people behind every space adventure: not the ones in the
capsule, but the ones in the room who never let them down.

Behind every hero in space, there is a team on the ground. Be ours.

[Your name] — MARS UCLouvain
