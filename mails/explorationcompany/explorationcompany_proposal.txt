Subject: Europe's capsule comes home. So does our crew. Bring the two together.

Dear The Exploration Company team,

Somewhere over the Mojave Desert, a capsule fell out of the sky, opened its
parachutes, and landed in the dust. It was a test of the vehicle that will one
day bring cargo — and perhaps people — home from space. Its name: Nyx. Its
flag: Europe's.

In a world of giants, you chose to build the return. Nyx is modular, reusable,
launcher-agnostic, powered by green propellants, designed to fly again and
again — a European answer to the question of how we bring things back from
orbit, built by a team of a few hundred people who decided that Europe could
write its own new space age. You are proof that a bold idea, born in Munich
and raised on test stands, can become the continent's bridge between orbit
and Earth.

We are doing something similar — with stories instead of cargo.

We are MARS UCLouvain, a student space programme from UCLouvain in Belgium.
Every two years, we send a crew of students to the Mars Desert Research
Station in the Utah desert — one of the most faithful Mars analogues on Earth.
For two weeks they live in isolation, run real experiments in health, biology,
geology and technology, and bring the mission back to classrooms across the
country. Since 2013, dozens of research projects have crossed the Atlantic
with us.

Your capsule's job is to return what orbit holds. Our crew's job is to return
what the desert holds: the science, the images, the stories, the proof that
exploration belongs to Europe's youth. Nyx is built to be reused, refuelled,
flown many times. Our story is the same — an idea that returns to classrooms
year after year, refuelled by each new crew, landing each time a little
closer to the next generation. Europe's return ticket to space, and Europe's
return ticket to inspiration: two vehicles, one direction.

Our challenge to you: put your name on our mission jersey and let our crew
carry a piece of Nyx — a patch, a story, a small piece of the European return
— to the desert and back. Then we will take it into classrooms across the
country and tell the next generation: a European spacecraft is being built to
come home, and you can be the ones who build the next one. Europe does not
need to dream smaller. It needs to dream European.

You bring Europe's cargo home from space. Help us bring Europe's youth home
to space.

[Your name] — MARS UCLouvain
