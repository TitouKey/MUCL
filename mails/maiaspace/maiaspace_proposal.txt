Subject: Europe's next big thing isn't the biggest rocket. It's the one that comes home.

Dear MaiaSpace team,

Somewhere in the Atlantic, one day soon, a European first stage will fall out
of the sky, light its engines, and land on a barge — like a giant has done
across the ocean for years. Except this time, it will be ours. Europe's. Yours.

You are the successor. Born in 2022, built in test-and-learn mode, aiming to
launch from the old Soyuz pad at Kourou barely five years after your creation.
Reusable. Eco-responsible. Powered by bio-methane. Europe's first reusable
mini-launcher, built by people who decided that the future of European space
would not be written by someone else.

While the giant across the Atlantic made reusability famous, you are proving
that Europe can learn the lesson younger, faster and greener — that a rocket
can come home, and that coming home is not a dream, it is engineering. For a
whole new generation, you are Europe's answer to the future. You are the
"SpaceX" of Europe — not a copy, a generation of your own.

We are that same generation, on the ground side. We are MARS UCLouvain, a
student space programme from UCLouvain in Belgium. Every two years, we send a
crew of students to the Mars Desert Research Station in the Utah desert — one
of the most faithful Mars analogues on Earth. For two weeks they live in
isolation, run real experiments in health, biology, geology and technology,
and bring the mission back to classrooms across the country. Since 2013,
dozens of research projects have crossed the Atlantic with us.

You are the new generation of European rockets. We are the new generation of
European space people. The ones who will cheer your first booster landing from
a grandstand, and who will tell every student in every classroom that the
future of space is not something we wait for — it is something we build, in
Europe, now.

Our request to you: as part of a partnership, would it be possible for our
crew to attend one of your test campaigns or launch activities at Kourou, or
to visit your facilities in France? We want to show that the young generation
supports the young generation — that what you are building is seen, believed
in, and that the next generation of European space engineers is already
watching, and already ready.

You are building the rocket that comes home. We are building the people who
will welcome it.

[Your name] — MARS UCLouvain
