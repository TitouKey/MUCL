Subject: You built the module taking humans back to the Moon. Let us show you what comes next.

Dear Airbus team,

In April 2026, four astronauts flew around the Moon for the first time in over
fifty years. They stayed alive because of air and water, propulsion and power
— all delivered by a module built in Bremen, by you. The European Service
Module is the heartbeat of Orion, and Airbus built it.

That module carries astronauts back to the Moon. Ours carries students to the
frontier of a different kind.

We are MARS UCLouvain, a student space programme from UCLouvain in Belgium.
Every two years, we send a crew of students to the Mars Desert Research
Station in the Utah desert — one of the most faithful Mars analogues on Earth.
For two weeks they live in isolation, in a habitat that keeps them alive the
same way your module keeps Orion alive: power, water, air, a crew trusting the
systems around them. They run real experiments in health, biology, geology and
technology, supervised by UCLouvain researchers. Since 2013, dozens of
research projects have crossed the Atlantic with us.

Your module is the ancestor of the habitation that will one day cross to Mars.
Our habitat in the desert is the rehearsal. The students we send there are the
engineers who will one day operate your modules, maintain them, build the next
ones. Right now, most of them are in classrooms where Airbus is just a name on
a news article.

Our challenge to you: put your name on the mission jersey worn through the
Utah desert, and let us introduce the next generation to the company that
took humanity back to the Moon — in person, in their classrooms, with the
story of Bremen and Orion told by students who are living their own space
mission. Or go further: let our crew test the habits your module relies on —
thermal control, life support, power in extreme isolation — in the harshest
conditions a student mission can reach.

You built the module that took humanity further. Help us build the ones who
will follow.

[Your name] — MARS UCLouvain
