Subject: There's a window in space with humanity's best view. It was built in Turin.

Dear Thales team,

Four hundred kilometres above our heads, there is a room with seven windows
and the best view ever given to a human being. On one side, the Earth, whole,
blue, fragile. On the other, the black of space. It is called the Cupola, and
it was built by you — as were most of the habitable volumes of the
International Space Station.

Our crew grew up looking at that window. And then we decided to build a small
version of it, on Earth.

We are MARS UCLouvain, a student space programme from UCLouvain in Belgium.
Every two years, we send a crew of students to the Mars Desert Research
Station in the Utah desert — one of the most faithful Mars analogues on Earth.
For two weeks they live in isolation, in a habitat, running real experiments in
health, biology, geology and technology. Since 2013, dozens of research
projects have crossed the Atlantic with us, supervised by UCLouvain
researchers. Our habitat has no orbit and no Cupola — but it has what matters:
a crew far from home, surviving on the systems around them, doing science.

You build the places where people live in space. We build the people who will
one day live there. Every student who returns from the desert and walks into a
Master's thesis or a PhD in aerospace engineering is, in part, your future
workforce — and we make sure they carry your story into classrooms across the
country.

Our challenge to you: put your name on our mission jersey, worn through the
desert, and become part of the smallest Thales habitat ever — or lend us your
expertise in the systems that keep a habitat alive: power, communications,
navigation, life support. Show a new generation that the places where humanity
lives beyond Earth are not built by magic. They are built by Thales.

We'll bring the crew. You bring the systems. Deal?

[Your name] — MARS UCLouvain
