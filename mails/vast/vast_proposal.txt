Subject: Your station runs on two-week missions. So does our crew.

Dear Vast team,

In a cleanroom in Long Beach, a module is being assembled that could become
the first commercial space station in history. One module. One great window.
Four crew members. Missions of two weeks. It is not the biggest station ever
imagined — and that is exactly the point. Haven-1 is built to fly, to learn,
to be the first step. Vast did not wait for perfection; you flew a demo
satellite, deorbited it safely, and kept going. Small, fast, real. That is how
space is being opened to a new era.

We recognised ourselves in that story before we even reached out.

We are MARS UCLouvain, a student space programme from UCLouvain in Belgium.
Every two years, we send a crew of students to the Mars Desert Research
Station in the Utah desert — one of the most faithful Mars analogues on Earth.
For two weeks they live in isolation, in a small habitat, running real
experiments in health, biology, geology and technology, supervised by
UCLouvain researchers. Since 2013, dozens of research projects have crossed
the Atlantic with us.

Two weeks. A small space. A crew that must live, work and return. When the
first astronauts step aboard Haven-1, they will live the exact rhythm our crew
has been rehearsing in the desert for years: isolation, limited room,
research, return. You are building the hardware of the two-week mission. We
are training the humans of the two-week mission. Minimum viable station,
minimum viable crew — same philosophy, different element.

Our challenge to you: put your name on our mission jersey, and let our desert
missions be the ground-level companion to your orbital ones. In our
classrooms, we will tell the next generation the story Vast is writing: that
the future of space is not built by giants alone, but by small, fast, brave
teams who choose to build and fly before they are perfect. Show them a station
being born, and show them that they too can start small — very small — and
still reach orbit.

You are building the world's first commercial station. We are building the
generation that will fill it. Two-week missions start here, on Earth.

[Your name] — MARS UCLouvain
