Subject: The computer a kid codes on at school is the same one that flies on the ISS. Send it to Mars with us.

Dear Raspberry Pi team,

There is a computer that lives in a million classrooms. A kid opens it, writes
a few lines, watches a light blink, and something clicks: *I can do this.*
The same small board, meanwhile, is running experiments 400 kilometres above
their head — Astro Pi on the International Space Station, letting school
children send code into orbit. No other computer in history serves that
purpose: the most unpretentious machine ever made, quietly turning the people
who use it into the people who build it.

That is the most powerful thing you have ever shipped. Not the chip — the
permission. You did not make computing cheap; you made it *yours*.

We are MARS UCLouvain, a student space programme from UCLouvain in Belgium.
Every two years, we send a crew of students to the Mars Desert Research
Station in the Utah desert — one of the most faithful Mars analogues on Earth.
For two weeks they live in isolation, run real experiments in health, biology,
geology and technology, and bring the mission back to classrooms across the
country. Since 2013, dozens of research projects have crossed the Atlantic
with us. And like you, we do not believe research belongs to institutions. Our
experiments are designed and built by students, with their own hands.

In the desert, a Raspberry Pi is the natural heart of the mission. Weather
stations in the red dust, soil and radiation sensors, timelapse cameras
watching the sky, a data logger keeping the crew's measurements alive, radio
links to mission control. One small board, doing what you taught the world
boards can do: everything. And when we come home, the same board becomes a
classroom lesson — because the machine our crew used in the desert is the
same machine a nine-year-old can program.

Our challenge to you: put Raspberry Pis at the centre of our mission, and put
your name on our mission jersey. Let a crew of students build the sensors,
run the code, and bring the whole story back to classrooms across the country
— proof, from the red desert, that the computer in every school can go
anywhere. You brought computing to the ISS through Astro Pi. Help us bring it
to the most Mars-like place on Earth, and tell a generation: if you can build
it, you can send it anywhere.

A small board. A huge idea. A mission. Let's build it.

[Your name] — MARS UCLouvain
