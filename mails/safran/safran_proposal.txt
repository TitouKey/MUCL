Subject: Every time humanity leaves Earth, your engines are the reason it can.

Dear Safran team,

Every time a rocket leaves Earth with people on board, there is a moment where
nothing matters except one thing: thrust. Escape velocity is not a metaphor.
It is a few thousand metres per second of pure, engineered violence — and for
decades, a large share of that force has been designed, built and perfected by
Safran.

That violence is what makes our story possible. Because of it, humans can
imagine leaving. Because of it, we can look at Mars and say: one day.

We are MARS UCLouvain, a student space programme from UCLouvain in Belgium.
We can't afford a rocket, so we do the next best thing: every two years, we
send a crew of students to the Mars Desert Research Station in the Utah desert
— one of the most faithful Mars analogues on Earth. For two weeks they live in
isolation, run real experiments in health, biology, geology and technology,
and bring the mission back to classrooms across the country. Since 2013,
dozens of research projects have crossed the Atlantic with us. Our propulsion
is an aeroplane, a car and a road through the desert. But the fire in the
story is the same one your engines light at every launch.

And there's something else we share. Safran powers rockets. Our mission powers
dreams — in the classrooms where we talk about how an engine works, why a
launch is a controlled explosion, and how the speed to reach Mars is decided
before the flame ever appears.

Our challenge to you: be the propulsion of our next expedition. Put your name
on the mission jersey that travels to the desert and back, or co-design with
us a school workshop that lets students build and fire their first "engines" —
turning the physics you master into something a child can hold. Give a new
generation the same gift you've always given humanity: the power to lift off.

Will you give our mission its first boost?

[Your name] — MARS UCLouvain
