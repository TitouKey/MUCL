Subject: From the cockpit to the wrist — be the heartbeat of our mission.

Dear Garmin team,

Few brands live in two such different worlds. At 30,000 feet, in a glass
cockpit, a pilot glances at a Garmin screen and trusts it with the lives of
everyone on board. On the ground, a runner glances at a Garmin watch and
trusts it with something smaller but just as important: the number that tells
them they can keep going.

High above the clouds, and in the most human of places — a wrist. That is
where Garmin lives. And that is exactly the range we need.

We are MARS UCLouvain, a student space programme from UCLouvain in Belgium.
Every two years, we send a crew of students to the Mars Desert Research
Station in the Utah desert — one of the most faithful Mars analogues on Earth.
For two weeks they live in isolation, run real experiments in health, biology,
geology and technology, and bring the mission back to classrooms across the
country. Since 2013, dozens of research projects have crossed the Atlantic
with us.

For us, following the data is not a luxury. It is everything. In the desert,
a heartbeat, a night of sleep, a stress level are not numbers on a screen —
they are how a crew in isolation knows it is still okay. Our research, our
training and our safety all depend on monitoring ourselves day after day. When
it came to choosing who would measure us, our eyes turned to you. You are the
ideal partner, and you always have been.

But for us, a Garmin is not only for the mission. It is there from the first
day of training to the last day of the expedition — in the victories and in
the failures. It is the companion that never lets us down. On the good days,
it quietly records what went right. On the bad days, it gives us a little
kick, a small honest number that says: get up, you can do this, go again.
From the beginning of the training to the end of the mission, it is the one
thing that stays on our wrist and never leaves us.

And that is still not enough. Because training is not a mission — training is
a life, lived every day. A healthy mind in a healthy body. We are not only
building a generation of brilliant students; we are building a generation of
students who are strong, who are fit, who are healthy enough to carry the
dreams they build. That is why we want you inside the university, among us:
so that Garmin is where the next generation lives, trains and grows — and so
that the young people who will one day explore the world know, early, that
their own body is the first machine they must maintain.

Our challenge to you: put your watches on our crew's wrists, from the first
training session to the last day in the desert — and your name on our mission
jersey. In return, you'll measure a crew at its limits, in the most demanding
environment a student mission can reach, and your story will travel with us
into classrooms across the country. Show a new generation that the watch on
their wrist is not a gadget. It is the companion that keeps them going — on
good days, on bad days, and all the way to Mars.

We'll bring the mission. You bring the measure.

[Your name] — MARS UCLouvain
