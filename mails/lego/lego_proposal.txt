Subject: Your next mission is waiting. Our crew is too.

Dear LEGO team,

Somewhere in a bedroom, a kid is pressing the last brick onto a rocket's nose
cone. It's Saturn V. It's 2 AM. Tomorrow morning, they'll carry it to school
and announce to the whole class: "One day, I'm going to space."

That kid is us. That kid is every single member of our crew.

We are MARS UCLouvain, a student space programme from UCLouvain in Belgium.
Every two years, we send a crew of students to the Mars Desert Research Station
in the Utah desert — one of the most faithful Mars analogues on Earth. For two
weeks they live in isolation, run real experiments in health, biology, geology
and technology, and bring the mission back to classrooms across the country.
Since 2013, dozens of research projects have crossed the Atlantic with us —
supervised by UCLouvain researchers, many of them starting Masters theses and
PhDs.

Our road to the desert began, like yours, with bricks. Saturn V. The Lunar
Lander. The International Space Station. And now the Tintin Moon Rocket — the
red-and-white rocket that taught an entire generation that space was not a
dream, but a plan. LEGO has built more rockets for more children than any space
agency ever will. And the kids who built them are now the ones building the
real thing.

You already know how to turn an adventure into a story millions watch. At the
last GP Explorer, the LEGO Racing Team stepped onto the podium with Maxime
Biaggi, in front of the largest audience French motorsport has ever seen — and
finished second in the team standings. You didn't just field a team. You became
part of a moment, a challenge, a story that thousands of kids will remember for
years.

So here's the thing. We have a habitat in a desert. We have a crew, a two-week
mission, a school programme. What we're missing is the brick it all started
with.

Our challenge to you: send a minifigure — a flag, your logo on the mission
jersey, anything — with us to the Utah desert. It will live in a Mars habitat,
sleep in a capsule, and come back with a signed story of its own, told in
classrooms across the country. We'll make sure every kid who ever built a LEGO
Saturn V knows their next destination is closer than they think.

Will you build this one with us?

[Your name] — MARS UCLouvain
