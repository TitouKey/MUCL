Subject: You're building where humanity will live next. We're training the ones who will live there.

Dear Axiom Space team,

Somewhere in Houston, a spacesuit is being fitted for a walk on the Moon. And
in the same city, a station is being assembled — the one that will carry
human presence in low Earth orbit after the International Space Station is
retired. When the story of humanity in space was about to be interrupted, you
were the ones who wrote the next chapter: the successor station, and the suit
that will leave new footprints in lunar dust.

Axiom does not just build hardware. You build the future of living and working
beyond Earth. And that is exactly what we are building, on a much smaller
scale — in a desert.

We are MARS UCLouvain, a student space programme from UCLouvain in Belgium.
Every two years, we send a crew of students to the Mars Desert Research
Station in the Utah desert — one of the most faithful Mars analogues on Earth.
For two weeks they live in isolation, in a habitat, running real experiments
in health, biology, geology and technology, supervised by UCLouvain
researchers. Since 2013, dozens of research projects have crossed the Atlantic
with us.

Here is the parallel we can't ignore. You test the AxEMU in simulated lunar
gravity, in pools and pressure chambers. We test ourselves in simulated
Martian isolation, in the red dust of Utah. You build the modules where humans
will live in orbit; we train the humans who will one day board them. When
Axiom Station opens its hatches to its first long-term crew, the people
operating it will be drawn from a generation that is, right now, sitting in
university classrooms — and some of them are ours.

Our challenge to you: put your name on our mission jersey, and give our crew a
small piece of Axiom to carry to the desert — a patch, a piece of the story.
We will bring it home signed, and take it into classrooms across the country
to tell students: here is the station being built, here is the suit that will
walk on the Moon, and here is the road you can take to be part of it. If you
would open a door for our crew to see the station hardware or your mission
operations — even once, even virtually — it would be the strongest lesson we
could offer the next generation.

You build where humanity lives next. Let us build the ones who will open the
door.

[Your name] — MARS UCLouvain
