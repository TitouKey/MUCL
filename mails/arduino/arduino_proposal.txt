Subject: The board that taught a generation "I built that." The desert is waiting.

Dear Arduino team,

Somewhere in a classroom, a student connects a board to a breadboard, a
sensor and a motor. A few minutes later, the motor turns. The student looks
up and says something they have never said before: *I built that.* That moment
happened in a design studio in Italy first — where Arduino was born not as a
product, but as a gift to students who wanted to control the physical world
without an engineering degree. Then it spread to every corner of the planet.

Arduino did not just make electronics. You made electronics *honest*: open
schematics, open code, no black boxes. You gave a generation permission to
open things, understand them, and make their own. The first robot, the first
sensor, the first time a young person made the physical world obey a line of
code — that is your legacy, and it is the same legacy we are building.

We are MARS UCLouvain, a student space programme from UCLouvain in Belgium.
Every two years, we send a crew of students to the Mars Desert Research
Station in the Utah desert — one of the most faithful Mars analogues on Earth.
For two weeks they live in isolation, run real experiments in health, biology,
geology and technology, and bring the mission back to classrooms across the
country. Since 2013, dozens of research projects have crossed the Atlantic
with us. Our crew is not a team of electronics engineers — it is a team of
students from every faculty, which is exactly why we need a platform anyone
can pick up.

In the desert, Arduino is where our ideas become instruments. Soil probes
measuring the ground that mimics Mars, sensors tracking temperature and dust,
small robots crawling the red terrain, data loggers keeping watch while the
crew sleeps. Open hardware, in the most open place a student can reach — and
when the mission ends, every circuit comes home and becomes a lesson: the
same board, back in a classroom, doing science again.

Our challenge to you: put Arduinos at the heart of our mission and put your
name on our mission jersey. Let our students build the desert's instruments,
let the boards survive the dust and the cold, and let us bring the story back
to schools across the country — telling every kid the words you gave the
world: *you can build that.* You started with students in a design studio in
Italy. Help us continue that story in a desert that looks like Mars.

Open hardware. Open minds. A mission. Let's make it together.

[Your name] — MARS UCLouvain
