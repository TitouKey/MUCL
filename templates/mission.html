{{ t('Three faces of one mission') }}

{{ t('A mission of inspiration') }}

{{ t('Space has always been a promise: to go further, to understand more, to dream bigger. When students live and research in a habitat in the desert, when the door opens on a landscape that resembles Mars, the promise becomes visible. For a pupil, for a student, seeing a university crew leave for the red planet is living proof that the extraordinary is possible — and that it begins here, today. Inspiration is not a side effect of the mission; it is its first goal.') }}

{{ t('A mission of demonstration') }}

{{ t('The mission is also a laboratory in action. Every experiment is designed, conducted and interpreted in the conditions of the field, live, as research really happens — with its doubts, its adjustments, its successes. Science leaves the pages of papers and becomes tangible: a habitat, spacesuits, a crew testing what a university does with knowledge. To present research is to show what it is worth: the power to turn ideas into progress.') }}

{{ t('An instrument of communication') }}

Because it is realistic, the mission carries an exceptional power of communication. It gives a face to research, a story to science, a body to the values of UCLouvain. Every expedition echoes far beyond the desert - in classrooms, in the media, in the public space - and repeats one conviction: exploring is learning, and learning is moving forward.

{{ t('The mission') }}

{{ t('A multifaceted mission') }}

{{ t('The mission is multiple. Academically, it carries research across a wide range of fields — health and biology, geology, physics and technology. For many crew members, it is a first taste of real academic research: experiments are designed, prepared and supervised like genuine scientific projects, under the guidance of UCLouvain researchers. Some students continue this work within a Master\u2019s thesis or a PhD, making the desert a starting point for their academic career.') }}

{{ t('Personally, it is a demanding experience that pushes every crew member to grow, to overcome their limits and to discover what they are capable of. Living and working in isolation, as a team, teaches responsibility, leadership, rigor and humility — skills that remain long after the mission is over.') }}

Pedagogically, it raises awareness, runs activities and inspires the next generation. It shows that we care about education and research - because the future of humanity matters. To better understand where we are going, we must first understand where we come from.

{{ t('A vision beyond Mars') }}

{{ t('Although the programme focuses on Mars, it is really about space as a whole. Mars is not an end in itself — it is the horizon that drives every step we take forward.') }}

{{ t('Every field trip, every experiment and every workshop is a milestone on a longer road. The technologies tested in the desert — habitats, life support, navigation, robotics, geology — find applications far beyond space, in medicine, energy, materials and the environment.') }}

{{ t('If the ambition is to reach Mars, we will accomplish a great deal along the way. Always aim for the Moon — even if you miss, you land among the stars.') }}

{{ t('A pedagogical dimension') }}

{{ t('Beyond the desert, the mission continues in classrooms. We design activities, talks and workshops that bring space, science and engineering to students of every age.') }}

{{ t('From primary schools to universities, our crews share what they lived: how an experiment is built, how a crew works, how decisions are made in isolation. Pupils follow the mission live, ask their questions and sometimes hold the models we built in the desert.') }}

{{ t('Teachers receive ready-to-use materials that connect the curriculum to the adventure — physics through flight, chemistry through life support, biology through astronaut health. Education becomes a launch pad.') }}

{{ t('Every intervention is a chance to spark curiosity — because the explorers of tomorrow are the children of today.') }}
