Dear Airbus team,

In April 2026, four astronauts flew around the Moon for the first time in over fifty years. They stayed alive because of air and water, propulsion and power — all delivered by a module built in Bremen, by you. The European Service Module is the heartbeat of Orion, and Airbus built it.

That module carries astronauts back to the Moon. Ours carries students to the frontier of a different kind.

We are MARS UCLouvain, a student space programme from UCLouvain in Belgium. Every two years, we send a crew of students to the Mars Desert Research Station in the Utah desert — one of the most faithful Mars analogues on Earth. For two weeks they live in isolation, in a habitat that keeps them alive the same way your module keeps Orion alive: power, water, air, a crew trusting the systems around them. They run real experiments in health, biology, geology and technology, supervised by UCLouvain researchers. Since 2013, dozens of research projects have crossed the Atlantic with us.

Your module is the ancestor of the habitation that will one day cross to Mars. Our habitat in the desert is the rehearsal. The students we send there are the engineers who will one day operate your modules, maintain them, build the next ones. Right now, most of them are in classrooms where Airbus is just a name on a news article.

You built the module that took humanity further. Help us build the ones who will follow.
